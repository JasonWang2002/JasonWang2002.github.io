About

Hi, I'm Jason! I'm a student in Vanderbilt's Medical Scientist Training Program with a background in biophysics and math. I've spent the last few years moving between wet-lab biophysics and more computational, machine-learning-heavy projects, and I'm now digging into deep learning in the Oliver lab.

Broadly, I'm interested in how modern ML can work alongside basic science to move the needle on real medical problems, rather than being an end in itself. On the clinical side I'm drawn to psychiatry, so I'm curious about where protein dynamics, structural biology, and neuroscience might eventually meet.

Research interests

My PhD work is aimed at designing proteins with specified dynamics that interact with specified targets. Most design methods still treat proteins as rigid, single-state objects, even though function often depends on how a protein moves.

Projects

Geometric deep learning for dynamics-aware protein design

Oliver Lab, Vanderbilt · Sep 2026–present

Protein function depends on conformational dynamics, yet most design methods ignore them. This project builds geometric deep learning models that describe protein dynamics as temporal changes in graphs of local residue contacts. The models will be trained on a lab-curated database of high-quality, structurally diverse proteins and then used in a design pipeline that proposes sequences with specified dynamical behavior and target interactions.

Water placement on cyclin-dependent kinases

Wankowicz Lab, Vanderbilt · Summer 2025 rotation

Identified patterns of water molecule placement on CDKs to inform the design of more targeted inhibitors.

AlphaFold2 conformations for bifunctional molecule design

Meiler Lab, Vanderbilt · Summer 2024 rotation

Used modified versions of AlphaFold2 with edited inputs to produce conformations of protein-protein interactions for bifunctional molecule design.

Optical trapping of vesicles

Baumgart Lab, University of Pennsylvania · 2021–2024

Used optical trapping to measure physical properties of vesicles. Wrote and documented the LabVIEW code that runs the trap, and developed bead-tracking and particle-detection algorithms for microscopy images.

Topology of the native state and protein folding rates

NSF REU, University of Tennessee at Chattanooga · Summer 2021

Computed quantitative geometric and topological measures of native protein structures in Python and correlated them with folding rates. This work led to my first paper (see below).
