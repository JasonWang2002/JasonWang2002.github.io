About

I'm a student in Vanderbilt's Medical Scientist Training Program with an interest in developing computational methods for tying protein structure to function, designing new drugs, and engineering proteins. On the clinical side, I want to pursue psychiatry.

Research interests

My PhD work is aimed at using computational tools in protein dynamics to probe the physical mechanisms underlying fundamental molecular processes in neuroscience.. I am integrating deep learning models and molecular dynamics simulations to identify hidden conformational states and intermediate structures of neurotransmitter transporters.

Broadly, I am interested in how simple interactions build into complex phenomena: atomic interactions driving large-scale protein conformational change, simple computations driving intelligent systems, and biopsychosocial factors driving complex psychiatric diseases.

Projects

Dynamics of the serotonin transporter

Oliver Lab, Vanderbilt · Sep 2026–present

Analyzing the dynamics behind serotonin transport, blockade, and release. Combining curated structures, molecular dynamics simulations, deep learning models to map residue motions, probe conformational landscapes, and identify novel ligands to modulate transport.

Water placement on cyclin-dependent kinases

Wankowicz Lab, Vanderbilt · Summer 2025 rotation

Identified patterns of water molecule placement on CDKs to inform the design of more targeted inhibitors.

AlphaFold2 conformations for bifunctional molecule design

Meiler Lab, Vanderbilt · Summer 2024 rotation

Used modified versions of AlphaFold2 with edited inputs to produce conformations of protein-protein interactions for bifunctional molecule design.

Optical trapping of vesicles

Baumgart Lab, University of Pennsylvania · 2021–2024

Used optical trapping to measure physical properties of vesicles. Wrote and documented the LabVIEW code that runs the trap, and developed bead-tracking and particle-detection algorithms for microscopy images.

Topology of the native state and protein folding rates

NSF REU, University of Tennessee at Chattanooga · Summer 2021

Computed quantitative geometric and topological measures of native protein structures in Python and correlated them with folding rates. This work led to my first paper (see below).
